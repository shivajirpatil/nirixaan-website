The gap

India built its own AI models. Very few have reached citizens.

The foundation is paid for. What's missing is a governed way to turn sovereign models into compliant, maintained citizen services.

01

More demand, same delivery model

Citizen platforms built on Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker and UMANG need constant upgrades, patches and support. Each new requirement adds integrator headcount and time.

02

Models need a path to production

Models alone don't deliver services. Departments also need retrieval over trusted documents, evaluation in each language, human review and secure connections to India Stack.

03

AI must now be auditable

AI used without inventory, risk tiering and a bill of materials is now a compliance liability for every department and every vendor.

Why now

Since 4 Nov 2026, every AI system must be auditable.

CERT-In's AI Security Auditing Guidelines v1.0 give every government department and vendor new obligations. The DPDP Rules phase in consent and data-handling duties on top.

An inventory and a risk tier for every AI system

Including AI features inside software you bought.

An AI bill of materials

Model provider, version and hash, base models, plugins. Switching provider means validating again.

Independent audit from tier 2 up

By an empanelled auditor at least yearly, with threat models covering prompts, retrieval, tools and agents.

Nirixaan Kavach produces all three as a by-product of the work: the inventory entry, the AI-BOM, the audit trail and the impact assessment, sealed into an evidence pack your auditor can check.

Design principles

How we build

Aligned with India's AI Governance Guidelines and their principle-based, techno-legal approach.

Sovereign by default

Data, models and logs stay in India, on infrastructure the government chooses.

Model-agnostic

No lock-in. As Indian models improve, departments switch to them through the router.

Evidence as a by-product

Audit material is generated while work happens, not assembled afterwards.

Autonomy earned in stages

Draft, then act with approval, then act alone, one workflow at a time.

People own the risk

Named officers approve scope, design, severity calls and anything outside the runbook.
